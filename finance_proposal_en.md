## 1. Introduction: The Core Principles of Currency Value Based on Supply and Demand

Let us begin with the absolute basics of economics: the law of supply and demand. We all know that prices are determined by this equilibrium. Yet, curiously enough, many contemporary fiscal and monetary experts seem to overlook or completely ignore this fundamental rule.

### 1.1 The Paradox of Intrinsic Value vs. Supply and Demand
Ask yourself, what is the most essential thing for human survival? The answer is oxygen. We can live without food for a month, and without water for a week, but take away oxygen, and we perish in minutes. Yet, this vital life infrastructure costs us absolutely nothing. Why? Simply because its supply is overwhelmingly vast relative to our demand. No matter how precious an item is, its market price drops to zero if the supply is limitless.

Now, consider Bitcoin. From a biological standpoint, it is non-essential for human survival. Humanity functioned perfectly fine without it for millennia. Yet, as of April 2026, Bitcoin trades at a staggering valuation exceeding 10 million Japanese Yen per unit. While it functions as a decentralized store of value (often called "digital gold") and carries speculative demand, its extraordinary price is purely a product of strictly limited supply balanced against strong market demand.

If intrinsic utility dictated price, oxygen would be infinitely more expensive than Bitcoin. But reality proves otherwise. The Japanese Yen (JPY) operates under these exact same dynamics. When the supply of currency is short, its value rises, causing deflation. When the supply is excessive, its value drops relative to goods, triggering inflation.

## 2. The Supply-Demand Structure of Fiat JPY and the Fallacy of "Issuance Methodology"

For fiat JPY, the supply factor governing its purchasing power is the total volume of currency circulating within the private economy—represented by monetary aggregates, specifically **M2 (Money Stock)**. 
On the demand side, currency demand is functionally proportional to the scale of economic activity, represented by **Gross Domestic Product (GDP)**, modulated by the velocity of money. 

Despite these clear mechanics, modern politicians, academics, bureaucrats, and financial experts remain captive to a dogmatic fallacy: the belief that the *method* of currency issuance is the primary determinant of inflation and currency debasement. This manifests in the rigid, legalistic prohibition of **Direct Finance (debt monetization / central bank financing)** under the assumption that only **Indirect Finance** prevents hyperinflation.

### 2.1 Correcting the Historical Misinterpretation: The Post-WWII Hyperinflation
The post-WWII hyperinflation in Japan is frequently cited as the definitive historical warning against direct finance. However, the root cause of this economic collapse was not the *method* of direct underwriting:
1. **Absolute Monetary Volume:** The issuance of currency was expanded to an extreme, absolute excess relative to economic output.
2. **Collapse of Real Supply Capacity:** Japan's physical productive capacity (factories, infrastructure) had been utterly devastated by wartime bombardment, resulting in an extreme scarcity of real goods.

Inflation and deflation are fundamentally determined by the aggregate volume of circulating money relative to real economic transaction demand, not by the accounting channel of its issuance. Whether via direct or indirect mechanisms, if the expansion of the money supply outpaces the growth of real demand, the value of the currency will depreciate. Therefore, the strategic focus of monetary policy must shift from the dogmatic restriction of issuance *methods* to the precise and transparent management of the *monetary aggregate (M2)*. While safeguarding currency issuance limits is vital, the dogmatic prohibition of direct finance causes profound structural harm under the current indirect finance framework.

## 3. The Neglect of Monetary Aggregate Management and the Limits of Asset-Backed Issuance

By managing currency supply (the total aggregate) based on currency demand, the value of a currency can be maintained and stabilized.
*   If inflation occurs, reduce the monetary aggregate.
*   If deflation occurs, increase the monetary aggregate.

This is the core foundation, yet current governments, the Bank of Japan (BOJ), and major global central banks fail to manage this directly; it is not even established as a policy target. This distortion stems entirely from the indirect finance system.
The contemporary global financial architecture attempts to manage currency value through an implicit adherence to "Asset-Backed Issuance (the collateral principle)" rather than the active management of supply and demand aggregates. This is a dogmatic continuation of the historical era when currency credibility relied on gold convertibility.

### 3.1 The Current Mechanism of Asset-Backed Currency Issuance
1.  **BOJ Issuance to Private Banks:** The BOJ extends loans to private banks secured by the banks' assets (with rare exceptions like unsecured pandemic relief loans), or purchases bank assets (such as government bond buying operations) and credits the proceeds to the banks' current accounts.
2.  **Private Bank Issuance to Corporations and Individuals:** Private banks extend loans backed by collateral, crediting the loan amount to the borrower's deposit account (credit creation).
3.  **BOJ Banknote Issuance:** When printing and issuing banknotes, the BOJ treats banknotes as a "liability account," conducting accounting entries as if asset backing were a mandatory prerequisite. Since the transition to the floating exchange rate system in 1973, BOJ notes are no longer convertible, yet they remain recorded as liabilities due to historical inertia.

### 3.2 The Circular Logic of "Backing That Fails to Back"
The philosophy that "currency holds value because it is backed by assets" contains a fundamental logical contradiction (circular logic). This is because the value of the underlying backing asset itself is denominated in that very currency.
For example, if a 100-million-yen real estate asset is used as collateral to secure a 70-million-yen loan, the underlying assumption is that real estate values remain relatively stable. However, during the collapse of the asset bubble, real estate values plummeted to one-fifth of their peak, leaving loans heavily under-collateralized and forcing numerous banks into insolvency. It is inherently contradictory to secure currency value using assets whose prices fluctuate wildly based on the expansion or contraction of that same currency. Similarly, when the BOJ issues currency backed by JPY-denominated Japanese Government Bonds (JGBs), it engages in identical circular logic; if the JPY becomes valueless, JGBs denominated in JPY simultaneously become valueless.

## 4. Resolving Fiscal and Monetary Dilemmas Through Monetary Aggregate Management (Direct Finance)

The core paradigm shift proposed herein states that "the value of currency is dictated not by asset backing, but by the equilibrium of supply and demand." To operationalize this reality, the following five structural reforms must be implemented:

1.  Manage inflation and deflation primarily through the active management of the monetary aggregate.
2.  Explicitly record and utilize seigniorage (currency issuance gain) within central bank accounting entries.
3.  Implement Direct Finance to manage the monetary aggregate directly rather than through indirect channels.
4.  Direct the allocation of issued currency toward the real economy, completely suppressing speculative money games.
5.  Incorporate private bank credit creation volumes into the framework of aggregate management target structures.

With outstanding national debt exceeding 1,200 trillion yen—more than double the nation's GDP—and a stagnation lasting 30 years, the structural distortions of the Japanese financial system have reached a critical point where superficial reforms are no longer viable.

### 4.1 Structural Flaws of the Current Indirect Finance Architecture
Under indirect finance, the government and the central bank cannot directly control fluctuations in "M2 (Money Stock)," which is the ultimate determinant of inflation and deflation.
*   **The Divergence Between MB (Monetary Base) and M2:** Under Abenomics, the BOJ expanded the MB multiple times, yet failed to escape deflation. In a stagnant economy, expanding MB does not automatically increase M2 because private banks withhold lending due to risk aversion (the collapse of the money multiplier).
*   **Uncertainty of M2 Expansion Pathways:** M2 expands either through fiscal deficits or private bank credit creation. However, under the current framework, newly issued government bonds must be purchased by private financial institutions or individuals. If funded by private money (M2), this act reduces circulating currency, neutralizing the stimulative effect of the fiscal deficit. The central bank cannot directly control how banks fund these purchases.
*   **Paralysis of Interest Rate Policy:** When central bank balance sheets are bloated with government bonds, raising interest rates to combat cost-push inflation induces massive unrealized losses on those bonds, risking central bank or private bank insolvency. Consequently, the BOJ becomes structurally powerless to defend currency value or restrict excessive M2 expansion through interest rate adjustments.

### 4.2 Wealth Disparity and the Speculative Money Game
Entrusting currency issuance entirely to private commercial banks creates a systemic defect often described as "lending an umbrella on a sunny day and stealing it back on a rainy day." Commercial banks naturally prioritize lending to credit-rich entities (large corporations and wealthy individuals) with substantial collateral, inflating currency where it is least required. The multiplier effect of such private bank lending on the real economy is estimated to be as low as 0.5.
This excess currency bypasses productive real-economy investment, flowing directly into speculative money games like real estate and equities, generating acute asset inflation. Consequently, the wealthy classes accumulate unearned asset gains while the real economy deteriorates, making widening wealth disparity an inevitable systemic outcome.

The BOJ records seigniorage within its ledger and utilizes a newly established asset account, "Japanese Yen (JPY Account)," to extend direct funding to the government. This mechanism allows the state to expand M2 directly, eliminating deflation instantaneously. A primary application is the distribution of monthly child allowances (helicopter money) funded entirely by seigniorage created *ex nihilo* by the central bank. Directing liquidity to consumer-heavy demographics stimulates authentic real-economy velocity.

### 5.1 Inflation Management and the "Automatic Brake Rule"
To insulate Direct Finance from the populist spending temptations inherent in democratic governance, the institutional independence of the central bank must be absolute and codified into law. Furthermore, this proposal advocates the implementation of a hardcoded **"Automatic Brake Rule"**: *If inflation indices cross a specific statutory threshold, all direct seigniorage disbursements to the government are instantly locked and suspended, bypassing and overriding any parliamentary or executive counter-resolutions.*

## 6. Historical Evolution of Modern Banking and the "Credit Creation" Taboo

### 6.1 Gold Deposits and the Alchemy of 17th-Century Goldsmiths
Modern commercial banking and paper currency originated with 17th-century London goldsmiths (Reference: Richard A. Werner, *Princes of the Yen*). To safeguard their bullion from arbitrary royal seizure, merchants deposited physical gold with goldsmiths, who issued paper "receipts." These receipts quickly began circulating as a convenient, trusted medium of exchange, functioning as the earliest paper currency.

The goldsmiths observed that depositors rarely withdrew their physical gold simultaneously and that their receipts functioned perfectly as currency. This realization allowed them to issue duplicate receipts far exceeding the physical gold stored in their vaults, lending them out at interest. This historic act established **fractional reserve banking, credit creation, and modern commercial banking**.

### 6.2 Theological Prohibitions and Institutional Secrecy
The practice of issuing receipts unbacked by physical gold and extracting interest directly violated scriptural prohibitions against usury within the community (Deuteronomy 23:20-21, Leviticus 25:35-37, Exodus 22:24). While the Reformation began permitting commercial interest, the boundary between legitimate commerce and usury remained highly controversial. To avoid profound social stigma and legal persecution, early bankers operated under extreme secrecy, institutionalizing a lack of transparency that shielded the mechanics of credit creation from rigorous public scrutiny for centuries.

Money embodies an inherent structural paradox: it must maintain scarcity to preserve value while simultaneously providing sufficient volume to facilitate commerce. Early fractional lending resolved this friction, unlocking the liquidity required to finance the Industrial Revolution (1760–1840).

### 6.3 The Suppression of Credit Creation within Mainstream Economics
The reality that private commercial banks create money out of thin air remained an academic taboo for generations. When Scottish economist Henry Dunning Macleod (1821–1902) formulated the credit theory of money, demonstrating that banks are creators of credit rather than mere intermediaries, he faced intense ridicule and ostracization from contemporary academic institutions. Mainstream economics dogmatically clung to the "Financial Intermediation Theory" (the fallacy that banks merely lend out existing deposits). As Richard Werner's empirical research demonstrates, this taboo was fiercely maintained, and central banking institutions only began explicitly acknowledging the mechanics of commercial credit creation after 2010. This protracted suppression delayed the realization of comprehensive monetary aggregate management.

## 7. Where Should the Money Be Spent?

Next, let us consider where the money should be allocated. Under a direct financing framework, the Bank of Japan (BOJ) can issue currency directly. In addition, private commercial banks issue currency through lending.

The question is: Where should this newly issued money go?

When it comes to fiscal expenditure, this is a paramount political question that demands complete transparency.

For instance, providing a government child allowance of 50,000 yen per child per month represents a major redistribution of income, requiring thorough democratic debate and public consensus.

However, equally important as the fiscal expenditure debate is the matter of commercial bank lending. Let us examine why.

### Competition and Significance of the Banking Sector

Currently, Japan's M2 money stock stands at roughly 1,300 trillion yen (of which approximately 120 trillion yen is physical cash), and roughly half of that corresponds to bank loan balances (over 600 trillion yen). It can be understood that M2 is generated roughly fifty-fifty between currency issued by the BOJ via fiscal deficits and currency created via commercial bank loans.

Thus, fiscal spending and bank lending carry equivalent weight regarding the fundamental question of "Where is the money spent?"

Under direct financing, currency issuance by the central bank to fund fiscal deficits and currency issuance by commercial banks through loans can be seen as competing forces. If bank lending expands, the money supply increases, creating inflationary pressure and reducing the capacity for fiscal deficits (though tax revenues rise, so the net nominal fiscal room varies). Conversely, if bank lending contracts, deflationary pressure intensifies, increasing the necessity for direct fiscal spending.

When viewed this way, while the central bank has the infinite capacity to issue currency, recording banknotes as a liability (without booking seigniorage as revenue) serves to weaken and suppress the public power of the central bank.

While modern Japanese bankers may not deliberately intend this, historically, central banks originated in 17th-century England as mutual aid institutions for private banks. One could reasonably suspect that this distorted accounting structure has been preserved so as not to interfere with the private banking business (currency creation).

Furthermore, the widespread dogma that "direct financing inevitably causes hyperinflation" is completely untenable under aggregate quantity management, raising the suspicion that it may have originated as propaganda disseminated by private banking interests.

In other words, by peddling the propaganda that direct financing triggers inflation, private interests could deceive politicians, suppress seigniorage accounting at the central bank, limit public money creation, and protect their exclusive, opaque privilege of lending out money they do not possess to earn interest.

While this may be the author's speculation, if true, resistance against these proposals from international financial elites is to be expected.

It is difficult to gauge how strong such resistance might be. However, looking at the cryptocurrency industry, existing international financial resistance appears far from insurmountable. For example, the SWIFT international payment network has been shaken by cryptocurrency and Ripple technologies. Although the SEC sued Ripple, it ended in a de facto victory for Ripple without any assassinations, and even the founder of Binance received a presidential pardon after his conviction.

In short, while institutional resistance may exist, it is not an absolute barrier that justifies sacrificing the Japanese or global economy.

Of course, private banks are not the enemy. Although the 17th-century goldsmiths' business model was somewhat opaque, their wide currency creation enabled economic expansion. Without them, liquidity would have been starved, capital accumulation thwarted, and the Industrial Revolution itself might never have occurred.

Nevertheless, private banks operate for their own profit, which is natural for private entities.

Therefore, commercial banking activities must be governed within the holistic framework of the entire economy. Ultimately, this is also for the banks' own survival; if the real economy collapses, the banking industry cannot survive either. Financial deregulation cannot simply be left entirely unchecked.

To return to the main point: How much the government spends and what it spends it on is of paramount importance. Controlling the quantitative volume and the qualitative direction of commercial bank credit is equally vital.

### Qualitative Credit Guidance for Bank Lending

Extending a loan grants the borrower newly created purchasing power. This purchasing power dictates what is produced, thereby shaping the overarching direction of economic activity.

### The Direction of the Real Economy

In terms of determining economic trajectory, targeting fiscal expenditure and targeting bank lending are equally consequential.

Merely imposing quantitative credit ceilings on private banks is insufficient (even if it manages broad inflation/deflation).

Certain loans are socially undesirable or flow into predatory industries, necessitating strict regulatory boundaries. Furthermore, speculative loans carry immense systemic risks.

Conversely, lending should be proactively encouraged for sectors that strengthen the real economy, elevate public well-being, and support employment in small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs).

To achieve this, banks must shift away from collateral-based lending toward project-finance evaluated on viable business plans.

The true raison d'être of banks lies in their risk evaluation and credit screening capabilities. Nuanced, localized credit assessments cannot easily be performed by a central government or central bank.

With the fiscal flexibility unlocked by direct financing, we should dismantle rigid collateral-dependence and pave the way for business-plan-based lending backed by robust government guarantees.

Of course, loans cannot be granted recklessly without accountability; viable evaluation standards remain essential.

For the banking system to sustain its institutional validity, deep discussions on lending destinations are indispensable.

SMEs underpinning the real economy represent the core of employment and innovation. Every giant corporation began as a fragile startup.

In contrast, asset-rich borrowers easily obtain loans due to heavy collateral, yet their borrowing exhibits low economic multiplier effects. Worse, it frequently fuels real estate speculation and financial money games, laying the groundwork for credit crises.

Hence, policy must prioritize loans directed toward productive capital investment in the real economy while restricting loans flowing into speculative bubbles.

### Practical Allocations of Capital (Three Core Pillars)

Continuing the discussion on where capital should be allocated, numerous applications exist, but here we highlight three critical pillars.

#### 1. Social Welfare and Basic Livelihood Protection

Many assume that welfare is a distant issue unrelated to them.

Regrettably, in modern society, anyone can face sudden unemployment. As AI and robotics proliferate, demand for conventional human labor will rapidly diminish.

Consider whether society is better off strictly restricting welfare—forcing desperate individuals toward illicit schemes and crime—or expanding welfare protections so everyone can live with dignity and security.

A fatal flaw in Japan's current welfare system is the rigid legal obligation placed on extended family members for financial support (*fuyō gimu*). This perpetuates tragedies where impoverished individuals starve in isolation, leaving behind notes pleading for a single rice ball.

Public assistance must be boldly expanded. Handing surplus funds to high-income earners yields minimal consumption; most is absorbed into savings or speculative assets without boosting GDP.

Conversely, allocating funds to low-income households directly stimulates consumption, generating high economic multiplier effects and expanding real GDP.

If geopolitical unrest triggers millions of boat refugees from neighboring regions, the immediate requirement will be food. Starving populations inevitably riot.

Furthermore, delivering international humanitarian aid in physical grain rather than cash prevents foreign despots from embezzling monetary aid.
